# Judging rubric

The common 2026 AI-hackathon split. **Presentation is the largest single bucket** — teams consistently under-invest in it because it feels less real than code.

| Weight | Dimension | What it actually measures |
|---|---|---|
| **30%** | Presentation | Does the 90s land? Is the story clear? Does the demo work live? |
| **25%** | Technical implementation | Is the AI use non-trivial? Does it work? Would an engineer be impressed? |
| **25%** | Problem-solving impact | Does this matter to real Haligonians? How many? What changes? |
| **20%** | Innovation | Is this obvious? Would three other teams build it? |

Halifax has extra prize tracks: **category winners** and **People's Choice (the room votes)**. People's Choice rewards delight and relatability, not rigor — see `senior-product-manager`.

## How to score

Give each dimension a 1–5 and multiply by weight. Then do the only part that matters:

> **Name the lowest-scoring dimension and spend the next hour there.**

Teams reflexively pour time into the 25% they enjoy (technical) while the 30% sits at a 2.

## Checkpoints

| Time | Ask |
|---|---|
| **9:50 kill gate** | Can this plausibly score 4+ on all four? If any dimension is capped at 2 by the *idea itself*, pick another idea — you cannot fix it later |
| **12:30 sync** | Which dimension is weakest right now? Reallocate |
| **14:00** | Score the actual demo, not the plan |

## Red flags that cap a dimension

- **Presentation capped** — no working live demo; presenter explains architecture instead of showing value; runs over time
- **Technical capped** — a chatbot wrapper; single prompt in, text out; the LLM does what a regex could
- **Impact capped** — solves a problem only engineers have; no real data; "could be used by anyone, anywhere" (i.e. not Halifax)
- **Innovation capped** — the first idea anyone would have for this challenge. See `ideation-grill-me`
